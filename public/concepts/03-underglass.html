What is moving

The market, from behind the counter

Christmas

German blown glass keeps climbing

Ornaments from the 1920s through the 1950s have been going up steadily. Buyers want originals, not the later reproductions that copied the shapes.

Bill's take

Check the cap and the wire hook first. That is where the fakes give themselves up.

Halloween

Pre-1960 pulp beats anything plastic

Demand got strong enough that new decorations are manufactured to look old. The people hunting the real thing have gotten good at telling the difference.

Bill's take

German pulp jack o’ lanterns walk out the fastest. Every year, same story.

Toys

Original paint over restoration

Buddy L, Hubley, Lionel and Tootsietoy all still trade steadily at auction. Honest wear on original paint holds value better than a clean repaint.

Bill's take

A repaint kills it. I would rather have the chips and the honest wear.

Behind the counter

Bill knows where every piece came from

He started in 1973 and has not stopped since. Most dealers write a price on the tag. Bill writes the year, the maker, the pattern, and whatever else he dug up, then ties it on by hand. Lisa runs the other half of the booth: the glass, the jewelry, the textiles, and the organizing that keeps a case full of small objects findable.

For most of those years it was his own thing. Then the kids got older and started leaving the house, and the weekends opened up. The two of them started filling them together, an estate sale on a Saturday and somebody's garage on a Sunday.

They talked about opening a shop for decades. Booth 18 is that conversation, finally, a long way from where it started. There are still New York plates hanging on the back wall of it.

What’s moving

The market, from behind the counter

Christmas

German glass is climbing

Blown glass ornaments from the 1920s through the 1950s keep going up. Buyers want the real ones, not the reproductions that flooded in later.

Bill’s take

Check the cap and the wire hook first. That is where the fakes give themselves up.

Halloween

Pulp and paper beat plastic

Demand for pre-1960 Halloween has been strong enough that new decorations are made to look old. People are hunting for the actual thing.

Bill’s take

German pulp jack o’ lanterns walk out the fastest. Every year, same story.

Toys

Original paint over restoration

Buddy L, Hubley, Lionel and Tootsietoy all still trade steadily at auction. Worn original paint holds value better than a repaint.

Bill’s take

A repaint kills it. I would rather have the chips and the honest wear.

The man at the counter

Bill knows where it came from

Most dealers write a price on the tag. Bill writes the year, the maker, the pattern and whatever else he dug up, then signs off on it. He has been doing it since 1973.

For most of those years it was his own thing. Then the kids got older and started leaving the house, and the weekends opened up. Bill and Lisa started filling them together. An estate sale on a Saturday. Somebody's garage on a Sunday. Two people in a car with coffee, driving out to look at other people's old things.

They talked about opening a shop for decades. Booth 18 is that conversation, finally, a long way from where it started. There are still New York plates hanging on the back wall of it, and Bill even lent a hand putting up booth walls when the mall was built. But listen to him talk about a piece for a minute and you understand the whole thing. In his words: “I learn the history of something when I learn about it. Because I can study it and then I’m pretty good at it.”

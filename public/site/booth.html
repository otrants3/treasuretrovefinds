The booth · Booth 18, Paradise Antique Mall

What's in the cases

"1920 to 1950 toys. Trucks, cars, trains. Buddy L, Hubley, Lionel, Tootsietoy diecast. Along with Christmas and Halloween from the 1920s to the 1960s."

Bill, asked what he collects

That is the spine of it. Around it sits everything else he could not walk past: bottles, advertising tins, dolls, kitchen things, tools.

House rule

All the cases are open. Ask, and anything comes out.

Walk this mall and you will find glass between you and most of the good stuff. Bill does it the other way. Point at a truck on the third shelf and it comes out, into your hands, so you can turn it over.

That is not a courtesy, it is the point. You cannot tell pressed steel from a reproduction across a pane of glass. You have to feel the weight of it and look at the underside.

Ask Bill about a piece

What things cost

The price is on the tag, in his handwriting.

Most of what is out sits between $10 and $100. A few standouts run higher, and the tag tells you that before you have to ask.

The tag is not just a number. Bill writes down what he found out: the maker, the year, what the thing actually is. You are reading his homework, tied on with string.

One piece, all the way down

A Gescha Spirit of St. Louis, Germany, 1928.

"This was made in 1928. Of course, Charles Lindbergh's flight was in 1927."

Bill, on tape at the mall

A wind-up, made in Germany the year after the flight it commemorates. Original, not a reproduction. A recent auction put one at $450.

That is what a record looks like. Not a guess about the era, but a date, a maker, a mechanism, and a sale to compare it to.

Check his work

He keeps the dating guides in the case, with magnifying glasses tied on.

The bottles run 1860 to 1900 and up. Instead of telling you what one is and asking you to take his word for it, Bill hands you the guide and the glass and lets you date it yourself.

It is the whole booth in one shelf. He did the homework, he shows the homework, and he is happy to be checked on it.

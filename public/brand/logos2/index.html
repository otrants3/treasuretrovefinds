Treasure Trove Finds · Brand marks, round two

Eleven logo concepts,
built on Long Island

Long Island is long and narrow, blunt at the west end, and it splits into two forks at the east. Put a bow on the west end and the whole island reads as a key, with the forks as the bit. One shape carries where Bill is from, the keyhole, and the key to a trove.

Every island lockup carries the line Long Island to Las Vegas, because a place silhouette should never have to identify itself. The words name the place, the shape is what you notice second, and the standalone marks stay wordless. Concept 11 is the control: the same geography with nothing at all to decode.

The island is not invented. Every control point is a real place converted from its latitude and longitude, checked against the OpenStreetMap coastline. Two shores are drawn wider than life so they survive 32 pixels: the North Fork, and the Napeague waist below Montauk. Colours are the booth palette. Type is Bricolage Grotesque and DM Mono, converted to outlines, so nothing here needs a font file.

Judge them small first. All eleven at 24 pixels, in a browser tab.

Concept 01

The island key

Long Island already looks like a key, so give it a bow and let it be one. The two forks are the bit, and the thing you unlock is the trove.

Concept 02

Keyhole island

An escutcheon plate off a display case, with the island as the hole you look through. Bill leaves his cases unlocked, so the mark says look, not keep out.

Concept 03

The island tag

Bill ties a handwritten tag to every piece, so the tag is the business. This one carries the island, and the red block is the price he writes last.

Concept 04

Chest and island

A trunk with the island as its lock plate, straddling the seam where the lid opens. The trove is literal here, which is the risk and the appeal.

Honest noteThe chest is the most expected shape in the set. It reads instantly and it argues least.

Concept 05

Island on a string

The island hung from a tag string like a charm, the way a small good thing comes tied to a bigger one.

Honest noteThe string and the jump ring are the thinnest lines in the suite. This one is a stationery mark, not a favicon.

Concept 06

The escutcheon

A maker's mark in a brass oval: where it comes from above, the way in below. Struck, not drawn, the way a foundry marked the base of old tin.

Concept 07

The forks

Only the east end, tapered to a point. It is Orient and Montauk to anyone from there, and an arrow pointing east to everyone else.

Concept 08

Lock and island

A padlock with the island cut clean out of the body. The heaviest, most stampable shape here, and the one that holds a square best.

Concept 09

Island monogram

Three letters sharing one crossbar, and the crossbar is the island. The forks run out past the F like a flourish.

Honest noteThe weakest small. Below about 40 pixels the three stems stop reading as T, T and F and start reading as a comb.

Concept 10

Key and tag

The key with Bill's tag tied to it. It is the whole promise in one object: the place, the way in, and the record that comes with it.

Honest noteTwo objects in one mark. Handsome at letterhead size, crowded below 48 pixels.

Concept 11

The wordline

The control. No island at all: the words do the geography, ruled above and below so the route is structure rather than a tagline. Nothing to decode.

Honest noteIt gives up the one shape that is only theirs. Safest to read, easiest to forget, and the mark alone says less than any island here.

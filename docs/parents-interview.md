# The conversation with Bill and Lisa

A question sheet for the sit-down. Record it on a phone if they will let you.
A recording is worth more than notes, because the site wants their actual
sentences, not a summary of them. Nothing on this list is filler. Every question
here is blocking something specific on the site, noted in brackets.

Rule that governs all of it: **we use their words or we use nothing.** If an
answer does not come, that spot stays a marked placeholder. We do not write a
nice-sounding line on their behalf.

---

## 1. Lisa, in her own voice

This is the biggest gap. Bill has a video and two transcripts. Lisa has one
sentence. The story section currently leans on him because that is all we have.

- How did you get into this? Were you into it before Bill, or did he pull you in?
  [story page, the Lisa half]
- What do you look for that Bill does not? You said jewelry, brooches, anything
  that catches your eye. What catches your eye?
- What is the best thing you have ever found, and where were you standing when
  you found it?
- What is a piece Bill wanted that you talked him out of, or the other way around?
- When you are at a sale together, who spots and who researches? Is that fair?
- What do you want people to feel when they walk up to the booths?

## 2. The two booths

We say 39 and 46 everywhere now, but we do not know how they differ.

- Why two booths instead of one? [booth page]
- What goes in 39 and what goes in 46? Is there a logic, or is it just space?
- When did you open? Month and year. [we are assuming 2025, unmarked]
- What is the mall's exact name on the sign?
- Can people browse your booths any day the mall is open, even when you are not
  there? [visit page says Wed and Sun, which may be reading as "closed otherwise"]
- The open-cases policy. Was that a decision, or just how you always did it?
  Whose idea? [this is the single best differentiator we have]

## 3. Bill, three short takes

Voice notes, thirty seconds each. These fill three marked placeholders in the
trove section. Ask him to answer as if a customer asked at the counter.

- What is one thing people always get wrong about old toys?
- What makes a piece worth real money versus worth ten dollars?
- What is in the booths right now that nobody appreciates enough?

## 4. The beginning

We currently say Bill has loved history since he was a kid on Long Island. Get
the real version.

- What is the first thing you ever collected, and how old were you?
- What year would you say you started, honestly? [the site avoids a start year
  because we are not sure. A confirmed year unlocks a real line.]
- Long Island to Las Vegas. When did you move, and what came with you?
  [the New York plates on the back wall are already in the photos]
- You two talked about a shop for years. What finally made you do it?

## 5. Read-back

Bring the story page up on a laptop and let them read it.

- Does this sound like you, or does it sound like a website?
- Anything in here you would not say out loud?
- Anything we left out that you would lead with?

## 6. Photos, before you leave

The one asset gating the top of the site.

- 5 to 10 shots of Bill facing the camera at the booths. Chest height, no flash,
  cases behind him, ideally holding a piece he can talk about.
- One of Bill and Lisa together. This does not exist yet and the story wants it.
- Ask if they will send a few photos every week of what is new. That feeds the
  planned "new in the booths this week" strip.

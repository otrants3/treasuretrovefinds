What is moving right now

Field notes · Updated monthly

Christmas

German blown glass keeps climbing

Ornaments from the 1920s through the 1950s have been going up steadily. Buyers want originals, not the later reproductions that copied the shapes.

Bill's take

Check the cap and the wire hook first. That is where the fakes give themselves up.

Halloween

Pre-1960 pulp beats anything plastic

Demand got strong enough that new decorations are now manufactured to look old. The people hunting the real thing have gotten good at telling the difference.

Bill's take

German pulp jack o’ lanterns walk out the fastest. Every year, same story.

Toys

Original paint over restoration

Buddy L, Hubley, Lionel and Tootsietoy all still trade steadily at auction. Honest wear on original paint holds its value better than a clean repaint.

Bill's take

A repaint kills it. I would rather have the chips and the honest wear.

Who you are buying from

Bill and Lisa Otranto

Bill started in 1973 and never stopped. He handles the toys, the trucks, the trains, the advertising tin, and the research that ends up on every tag. Lisa runs the other half: the glass, the jewelry, the textiles, and the part that keeps a booth full of small objects findable.

For most of those years it was his own thing, done on his own time. Then the kids got older and started leaving the house, the way kids do, and the weekends opened up. They started filling them together. An estate sale on a Saturday. Somebody's garage on a Sunday. Two people in a car with coffee, driving out to look at other people's old things.

Come by the house on any given night and Bill is at the kitchen table with a tag in one hand and a reference book in the other, working out what something is and what it is worth.

They talked about opening a shop for decades. It stayed a conversation, because life kept happening the way it does. Booth 18 is that conversation, finally, a long way from where it started. There are still New York plates hanging on the back wall of it. And when the mall itself was being built and the owner ran out of labor, Bill picked up a drill and helped screw the booth walls together. All 84 of them.

## a) Best 6 hero candidates

1. **photos/tag-perfume-bottle.jpg** - the filigree perfume bottle with the tag fully readable at "1934 Ludwig Scherk Renaissance Perfume bottle w/ Marie Antonette picture $225.00". Single subject, clean depth of field, and it proves the whole brand promise in one frame.
2. **photos/tag-race-car.jpg** - landscape close-up of the worn green Hubley race car with its $65.00 tag tied to the wheel. The only strong landscape hero in the set, so it is the natural wide banner.
3. **photos/case-toys.jpg** - the worn mohair teddy bear in saddle shoes in front of the Oshkosh trunk. Warm, human, and reads at any size.
4. **photos/booth-wide.jpg** - the best establishing shot of the booth. The "TREASURE TROVE FINDS" banner and the "GONE ANTIQUING" sign are both legible.
5. **cabinet/items/dress-form.jpg** - the canvas dress form against the wall of beer trays. Strong vertical subject with period signage behind it.
6. **cabinet/items/pepsi-crate-bottles.jpg** - twenty tagged antique bottles in a Pepsi crate. The single best picture of the tag system at scale, with several tags fully readable.

Close runners up if a slot opens: cabinet/items/teddy-school-desk.jpg, cabinet/items/pulleys.jpg, cabinet/items/cobalt-bottle.jpg, cabinet/items/brookins-can.jpg.

## b) Filenames that badly contradict their content

Six files are wrong enough that the filename must never be used as a label:

| File | Filename implies | What is actually in the photo |
|---|---|---|
| cabinet/items/oak-park-tin.jpg | a tin | two New York license plates, "703-FBX" and "5466-CT", with Star Trek action figures above. No tin at all. |
| cabinet/items/raggedy-anns.jpg | Raggedy Ann dolls | a shelf of kitchen gear: egg beaters, ice cube trays, jelly molds, a boxed Handy-Hannah electric hair dryer. No dolls. |
| cabinet/items/army-jeep.jpg | an army jeep | a green Hubley diecast race car tagged $65.00 with plastic army men around it. No jeep. |
| cabinet/items/yoda-c3po.jpg | Star Wars figures | a brass E.T. Fraim railway signal lock tagged $65.00 is the subject. Yoda and C-3PO are small and at the bottom edge. |
| photos/fan-perfumes.jpg | perfume bottles | the GE fan and a table of pewter and aluminum housewares. Zero perfume bottles. |
| photos/case-toys.jpg | a case of toys | a single teddy bear portrait in front of an Oshkosh steamer trunk. Not a case shot. |

Milder mismatches worth knowing:
- **cabinet/items/donald-duckling-tin.jpg** - the duck is vinyl, not tin. The tin pieces in frame are a spinning top and a Fisher-Price clock.
- **cabinet/items/steiff-menagerie.jpg** - the full-size file really is a menagerie (owl, koalas, lion), so the name fits. Any earlier note that it is a lone owl probably came from the thumbnail crop.
- **cabinet/items/perfume-bottle.jpg** - accurate, and it is the same object as photos/tag-perfume-bottle.jpg. Do not present them as two different pieces.
- **photos/tag-detail.jpg** and **photos/tag-race-car.jpg** are the same race car from two angles. Same caution.

## c) Too weak to use

- **cabinet/items/shasta-bitters.jpg** - out of focus. The crate stencil reads but the price card does not.
- **cabinet/items/crock-jugs.jpg** - soft focus throughout; the coffee tin brand is unreadable.

## Notes for whoever builds the page

- Nineteen of the 24 cabinet images and 5 of the 12 photos have at least one of Bill's handwritten tags visible. The four tag-closeup files are the ones where a tag is actually the subject.
- All 24 cabinet item images are square at 1200 by 1200. Of the photos, only photos/tag-race-car.jpg is landscape; the rest are portrait.
- Prices seen on tags in these photos run from $4.00 to $245.00, so any price the site quotes should be pulled from a tag that is legible in the image and nowhere else.

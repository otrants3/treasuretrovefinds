The story

It starts with a kid on Long Island.

Bill has loved history since he was a kid there. He has been collecting for decades, most of it on his own. Booth 18 is the part he and Lisa talked about for years before it happened.

The short version

Long Island. A kid who wanted to know about old things.

Decades of collecting. Most of it on his own.

The kids moved out. Bill and Lisa started hunting together.

2025. The shop they always talked about opened in Las Vegas.

Booth 18 · Las Vegas

Decades of it

He learns a thing by studying it.

For most of those years Bill collected alone. He would find something, and then he would go find out about it: who made it, what year, how it works, what one sold for at auction.

What he learned goes on the tag, in his handwriting, tied to the object. Other vendors write a price. He writes down the rest of it too, so the piece leaves with what he knows about it.

Bill and Lisa

The house got quiet, so they went looking.

The kids grew up and moved out and the weekends went quiet. Bill and Lisa started filling them together: estate sales, garage sales, anywhere old things turn up.

It became how they reconnected. Two people out early on a Saturday, looking through other people's attics.

For years they talked about opening a shop someday. It stayed talk.

Who runs it

Bill and Lisa Otranto. Both owners. Long Island, New York to Las Vegas, Nevada.

2025

Long Island to Las Vegas.

Then the talking stopped. In 2025 they took Booth 18 at Paradise Antique Mall on East Flamingo Road, unpacked decades of finds, and filled it floor to ceiling. Bill knows the building from the inside out, having helped screw together booth walls when the mall was built.

The mall holds 84 booths. Theirs is number 18, and there are still New York plates hanging on the back wall.

How they run the booth

The cases are open.

In a mall where most cases stay locked, Bill's open. Ask for anything and it comes out of the case and into your hands.

It is the same instinct as the handwritten tags. He would rather you look closely at a thing than take his word for it.
